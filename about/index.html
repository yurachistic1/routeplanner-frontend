About Runthe

A free running route planner for the UK. It's a personal project.

What it does

Loops

Pick where you start and how far you want to run, from 1 to 35 km. Runthe finds up to 50 different loops that bring you back to where you began. Look through them on the map, see the climb and elevation of each, and sort them by climb.

A to B

Tap points on the map and Runthe plans the way between them. Switch between a pleasant route, the shortest one and freehand as you go, keep the climbing down, and add laps.

GPS art

Choose a shape, a number or a word, or draw your own, and Runthe looks for streets that draw it.

How it picks routes

Runthe likes parks, canal paths and quiet streets. It avoids big roads, and crossing them, where it can, and loops come home a different way wherever there is one. Countryside paths that might be overgrown or muddy are marked on the map.

Where it works

Anywhere in the UK with streets or paths to run on. Nowhere else yet.

Good to know

  • You don't need an account. Sign in only if you want to save favourites or keep the links you share.
  • Any route can be downloaded as a GPX file, for your watch or another app.
  • Copy link makes a short link that opens the route for anyone.
  • It runs in your browser, and you can add it to your phone's home screen.
  • There are no adverts, analytics or tracking cookies. Privacy has the details.

Why I made it

I built the first version in 2021 as a university project. It made loops, and it was super basic. I came back to it because I didn't really like any of the route planners I'd tried, for loops or for point to point, and felt I could make one that suits me better.

The biggest difference, I think, is how many loops you get at once to look through and sort (by climb, for now). They're meant to be pleasant and easy to run, and to help you explore your neighbourhood, so they're for places you run all the time as much as places you don't know. I've lived in the same place for three years, and Runthe has still taken me down plenty of local streets that were completely new to me.

I'm also just really into loops. Some time ago I completed the Capital Ring in one day, an experience I never wish to repeat, but it is the ultimate ring in London.

I felt I could make a better point-to-point planner than the ones I'd been using, too, though maybe I just didn't look hard enough. Mine is about switching quickly between routing modes and freehand as you put points down, and being easy to use on a phone. GPS art came along the way as a cool little gimmick: I enjoy running those routes, but they're hard to plan.

So it's a personal project, but I've put quite a bit of time into it, and if you've come across it, I hope you enjoy using it. It's free for now. If it ever gets a lot of users I won't be able to cover the running costs, but we're not there yet, so I'm not too worried.
